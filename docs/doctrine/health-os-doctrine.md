# Impilo Health Operating System and Unified Experience — Foundational Doctrine

> **Canonical summary**: Impilo is a Health Operating System: a trusted, governed,
> extensible, and interoperable national digital environment in which health identities,
> records, workflows, services, transactions, intelligence, assets, and applications
> operate coherently. Its role-based unified UI/UX is the common experience shell of that
> operating system, allowing diverse actors to participate through one coherent interaction
> model while adapting safely to role, authority, setting, and task. Each natural person is
> anchored by one primary Client / Health / Impilo ID, while other lawful identifiers such
> as Provider ID, Facility ID, Product ID, Prescription ID, Asset ID, and Equipment ID are
> attached to their relevant identity, context, object, transaction, or infrastructure
> classes. In this way, Impilo preserves person-centered continuity while supporting
> multiple roles, multiple contexts, modular applications, and governed participation across
> the full continuum of health-related life and work.

> **Short doctrine line**: One Health Operating System, one experience shell, one person
> anchor, many roles, many IDs, many contexts, one governed runtime.

---

## 1. Foundational Doctrine

Impilo is conceived not merely as a digital health platform, but as a **Health Operating
System**: a trusted, governed, extensible, and interoperable national digital environment
within which health-related identities, records, workflows, transactions, services,
intelligence, assets, and applications can operate coherently.

This doctrine recognizes that health is not confined to isolated clinical encounters or
institutional transactions. Health spans prevention, wellness, illness, care, support,
monitoring, coordination, fulfilment, payment, regulation, public health action, and the
material conditions that make service delivery possible. Impilo must therefore be conceived
from the outset at the scale of real health-related life, even where implementation proceeds
in practical phases.

The Impilo Health Operating System provides the common runtime for this reality. It
establishes the shared trust, policy, identity, context, record, workflow,
interoperability, and extension services upon which health participation occurs and upon
which modular applications and services may run.

---

## 2. Experience Doctrine

The experience layer is the human- and actor-facing participation layer of the Health
Operating System. It is the environment in which the system's core transactions become
real.

In Impilo, the experience is what happens in and around the core transaction: the trusted,
contextual, role-aware, longitudinal interaction through which health-related value is
sought, offered, coordinated, delivered, recorded, fulfilled, and carried forward across
time.

This experience is inherently broad. It includes clinical care, self-care, caregiving,
service discovery, telemedicine, remote monitoring, wellness participation, referrals,
prescriptions, payments, public health functions, administrative work, and interactions
mediated by devices and intelligent agents. This breadth is not an implementation flaw to
be narrowed, but a domain truth to be acknowledged.

Implementation may begin with selected workflows, but the doctrine must remain broad enough
to accommodate the full continuum of health-related participation.

---

## 3. Health Operating System Doctrine

Impilo is called a Health Operating System because it is intended to provide more than a
set of applications or a marketplace for transactions. It is meant to provide the governed
execution environment in which multiple actors, systems, records, workflows, and apps
operate through common rules and shared services.

A platform enables participation and exchange. A Health Operating System goes further: it
provides the foundational environment in which participation, exchange, execution, control,
and extensibility are governed.

Accordingly, the Health Operating System must provide, at minimum:

1. Common identity and trust services
2. Policy and consent enforcement
3. Shared registries and canonical references
4. Longitudinal record and data services
5. Workflow, messaging, and event orchestration
6. Transaction and fulfilment rails
7. Audit and traceability
8. Standards-based interoperability
9. Extension points for modules, apps, and integrations
10. A coherent unified experience shell

The Health Operating System is therefore both an architectural model and a sovereignty
model. It positions Impilo as foundational digital public infrastructure for health rather
than as a single programme system, vendor application, or donor-specific tool.

---

## 4. Role-Based Unified UI/UX Doctrine

The role-based unified UI/UX is the common experience shell of the Impilo Health Operating
System.

It exists because a Health Operating System cannot present itself as a fragmented
collection of unrelated portals, identities, and workflows. It requires one coherent
interaction environment through which different actors can participate safely and naturally
according to role, authority, context, and task.

The doctrine is therefore:

> **One coherent experience shell, one primary person identity, many possible roles, many
> lawful attached identifiers, many contexts, many applications, one governed environment.**

"Unified" does not mean identical. Different workspaces may present different functions,
layouts, and priorities. A citizen-facing view, a provider-facing workspace, a facility
operations console, and a registry administration interface may differ substantially. But
they must still belong to the same governed experience world, share the same trust model,
and behave consistently as parts of one Health Operating System.

"Role-based" means the system adapts what is visible, enabled, required, or emphasized
according to the active role and context of the actor. This includes regulated professional
roles, caregiving roles, operational roles, administrative roles, and non-clinical
participation roles.

---

## 5. Identity Doctrine

Impilo is person-centered. Each natural person participating in the ecosystem shall be
anchored by **one primary Client / Health / Impilo ID**. This is the canonical health
identity of the person within the Health Operating System.

The Health ID establishes personhood, continuity, and longitudinal participation in the
health ecosystem. It is the anchor through which the system recognizes the same human being
across time, roles, institutions, and services.

However, one human being may lawfully hold multiple other identifiers linked to role,
organization, function, domain object, or transaction. These identifiers are not competing
persons. They are attached identifiers serving distinct semantic and governance purposes.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

- Provider ID
- Employee or Staff ID
- Facility ID
- Department ID
- Workspace ID
- Product ID
- Prescription ID
- Asset ID
- Equipment ID
- Claim ID
- Appointment ID
- Order ID
- Referral ID

The doctrine is therefore:

> **One person, one Health ID anchor, many attached identifiers of different classes, one
> governed active context at a time.**

---

## 6. Regulated Professional Participation Doctrine

A person may sign up or sign in to Impilo using their primary Health ID and associated
credentials. This establishes them as a recognized natural person in the ecosystem and
allows person-level participation in services appropriate to that base identity.

However, regulated health worker work within the experience layer requires additional
activation of a valid Provider ID and the relevant professional authority context.

Thus:

- The **Health ID** establishes *who the person is*
- The **Provider ID** establishes *the regulated professional capacity in which the person
  is acting*

Professional execution within the Health Operating System shall therefore require, where
applicable:

1. A linked and valid Provider ID
2. Appropriate licensure or registration status
3. Applicable organizational affiliation
4. Active operational context, such as facility or workspace
5. Appropriate entitlements and purpose of use
6. Elevated authentication where required

The doctrine may be summarized as:

> **Sign in as a person; practice as a provider only under activated Provider ID.**

---

## 7. Organizational and Operational Context Doctrine

No action in the Health Operating System should be understood only in terms of the person
acting. Many actions also depend on organizational and operational context.

Accordingly, the Health Operating System shall maintain identifiers and contextual
references for institutions, service points, workspaces, and operational settings,
including but not limited to:

- Facility ID
- Department ID
- Ward ID
- Programme ID
- Workspace ID
- Shift or assignment context

This ensures that the system can distinguish not only *who* is acting, but *where*, under
*what institutional authority*, and under *what operational circumstances*.

Thus, many governed actions may require a combination of:

- Person identity
- Role identity
- Organizational context
- Operational context
- Subject context
- Entitlement context

---

## 8. Multi-Class Identifier Doctrine

Impilo's identity doctrine is not limited to person identity. The Health Operating System
must maintain distinct but interoperable identifiers for multiple classes of entities.

At a minimum, these classes include:

### Actor IDs
These identify **who** is involved.
Examples: Health ID, Provider ID, Staff ID, caregiver linkage identifiers.

### Context IDs
These identify **where** or **under what organizational setting** an action occurs.
Examples: Facility ID, Department ID, Ward ID, Workspace ID, Programme ID.

### Object IDs
These identify **what thing, resource, or catalog object** is being referenced.
Examples: Product ID, Medication ID, Service ID, Asset ID, Equipment ID, Device ID.

### Transaction IDs
These identify **specific operational or clinical action instances**.
Examples: Prescription ID, Order ID, Referral ID, Appointment ID, Claim ID, Dispense ID,
Payment ID.

### Record IDs
These identify **persistent information objects**.
Examples: Observation ID, Consent ID, Document ID, Care Plan ID, Lab Result ID.

### Event IDs
These identify **discrete events in the runtime**.
Examples: Alert ID, Notification ID, Audit Event ID, Device Reading Event ID, Workflow
Event ID.

Each identifier class serves a different semantic function and must not be conflated with
another.

---

## 9. Asset and Equipment Doctrine

The Health Operating System must govern not only people, records, and workflows, but also
the material and technical infrastructure that supports service delivery.

Identifiers such as Asset ID and Equipment ID are therefore first-class components of the
doctrine.

- **Asset ID** identifies a managed asset from an ownership, deployment, finance,
  governance, or lifecycle perspective.
- **Equipment ID** identifies a specific operational device or piece of equipment in use
  for care, diagnostics, monitoring, support, or infrastructure.

This ensures that the Health Operating System can coordinate not only care and
administration, but also service readiness, maintenance, deployment, and device-linked
activities.

---

## 10. App and Extensibility Doctrine

Because Impilo is a Health Operating System, it must be structured to support modular
capabilities, applications, and integrations running on top of common core services.

This does not imply an uncontrolled consumer-style app store. It implies a **governed app
ecosystem** in which applications or modules may be:

- Registered
- Embedded
- Activated by role, facility, tenant, or programme
- Externally connected through approved APIs and event contracts

No application or module may bypass the core trust, policy, identity, context,
interoperability, or audit services of the Health Operating System.

All extensions must inherit or comply with the operating doctrine of the Health OS.

---

## 11. Access Control Doctrine

Access control within Impilo must not rely on simple role labels alone. It must be based
on a governed combination of:

1. Person identity
2. Active role
3. Attached role identifier (where relevant)
4. Organizational affiliation
5. Facility or workspace context
6. Subject relationship
7. Purpose of use
8. Consent or legal basis
9. Assurance level
10. Workflow state

This requires a model combining role-based access control (RBAC), attribute-based access
control (ABAC), relationship-aware controls, and break-glass mechanisms with elevated
justification and audit.

---

## 12. Audit and Traceability Doctrine

Because a single human may hold multiple roles and use multiple identifiers in different
contexts, audit and traceability must capture the full action context.

Every significant action should, where applicable, be traceable to:

1. The person anchor (Health ID)
2. The active role
3. The active role-linked identifier (e.g. Provider ID)
4. The organizational context (e.g. Facility ID)
5. The facility or workspace context
6. The subject of action
7. The transaction or record instance
8. The app or module used
9. The purpose of use
10. The time, channel, and event context

This ensures that accountability is grounded in both identity and context.

---

## 13. Design Consequences

The following design consequences arise from this doctrine:

1. Every natural person should be represented once in the health ecosystem through one
   primary Health ID.

2. The unified experience shell should preserve continuity of identity even where users
   move across multiple roles and workspaces.

3. Role activation, especially for regulated work, must be explicit and context-aware.

4. Different apps and modules should feel native to one coherent Health Operating System
   rather than like disconnected products.

5. The system must distinguish clearly between *who* is acting, *in what capacity*, under
   *what organizational authority*, using *which identifiers*, on *what subject*, and
   through *which transaction or record instance*.

6. The doctrine must accommodate broad, life-linked participation while preserving
   high-discipline control for clinical, regulatory, and operational work.
