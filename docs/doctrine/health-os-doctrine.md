# Impilo Health Operating System and Unified Experience — Foundational Doctrine

> **Canonical summary**: Impilo is a Health Operating System: a trusted, governed,
> extensible, interoperable, and person-centered national digital environment in which
> health identities, records, workflows, services, transactions, intelligence, assets,
> devices, communities, wellness experiences, dietary and sleep-related participation,
> searchable knowledge, conversational guidance, marketplace functions, and applications
> operate coherently. Its role-based unified UI/UX is the common experience shell of that
> operating system, allowing diverse actors to participate through one coherent interaction
> model while adapting safely to role, authority, setting, task, risk, device context, and
> lawful personalization. Each natural person is anchored by one primary Client / Health /
> Impilo ID, while other lawful identifiers such as Provider ID, Facility ID, Product ID,
> Prescription ID, Asset ID, Equipment ID, and Device ID are attached to their relevant
> identity, context, object, transaction, infrastructure, or connected-device classes.

> **Short doctrine line**: One Health Operating System, one experience shell, one person
> anchor, many roles, many IDs, many contexts, many experience modes, many connected
> entities, one governed **intelligent** runtime.

---

## 1. Foundational Doctrine

Impilo is conceived not merely as a digital health platform, but as a **Health Operating
System**: a trusted, governed, extensible, interoperable, and person-centered national
digital environment within which health-related identities, records, workflows,
transactions, services, intelligence, assets, devices, applications, and broader
life-linked participation can operate coherently.

This doctrine recognizes that health is not confined to clinical encounters, facilities, or
disease episodes alone. Health spans prevention, wellness, lifestyle, self-care, illness,
care, support, monitoring, social participation, fulfilment, payment, regulation, public
health action, and the material and digital conditions that make service delivery and
healthier living possible. Impilo must therefore be conceived from the outset at the scale
of real human life as it relates to health, even where implementation proceeds in practical
phases.

---

## 2. Experience Doctrine

The experience layer is the actor-facing participation layer of the Health Operating
System. It is the environment in which the system's core transactions and broader value
exchanges become real.

This experience is inherently broad. It includes, but is not limited to:

- Clinical care, self-care, caregiving, telemedicine
- Service discovery, medication access, remote monitoring
- Prescriptions, referrals, bookings, communication, payment
- Regulated and non-regulated marketplace participation
- **Wellness engagement, fitness participation, lifestyle support**
- **Diet and nutrition support, sleep and recovery support**
- **Clubs, communities, and peer groups**
- **Coaching and habit support**
- Public health functions, administrative work
- Interactions mediated by devices and intelligent agents

This breadth is not an implementation flaw to be narrowed, but a domain truth to be
acknowledged.

---

## 2a. Intelligent Experience Layer Doctrine

The Impilo experience layer is **intelligent**. It is not merely a passive interface for
viewing records, entering data, requesting services, or completing transactions. It is a
context-aware, knowledge-enabled, searchable, conversational, and action-supporting layer
that can receive information, interpret relevant signals, and respond to users with timely,
useful, and governed guidance.

The intelligent experience layer shall be capable of:

- Understanding who the actor is and in what role they are participating
- Using lawful context such as age, sex, life stage, location, health status, medications,
  goals, activity, diet, sleep, device data, exposures, and care plans
- Enabling search across governed health, wellness, lifestyle, diet, sleep, service, and
  marketplace knowledge domains
- Interacting through conversational and chat-based experiences
- Surfacing relevant education, reminders, prompts, alerts, and recommendations
- Supporting preventive, promotive, curative, rehabilitative, and wellness-oriented action
- Helping users discover services, communities, products, and knowledge relevant to needs
- Responding proactively where appropriate — adherence, screening, follow-up, campaigns,
  outbreaks, environmental risks, and healthy living support
- Operating within user preferences, consent settings, safety guardrails, and applicable
  legal and policy limits

> The experience layer is intelligent: it listens, understands, searches, responds,
> guides, reminds, alerts, and acts within governed boundaries.

---

## 3. Health Operating System Doctrine

The Health Operating System must provide, at minimum:

1. Common identity and trust services
2. Policy and consent enforcement
3. Shared registries and canonical references
4. Longitudinal record and data services
5. Workflow, messaging, and event orchestration
6. Transaction and fulfilment rails
7. **Marketplace rails**
8. **Device and IoT participation rails**
9. Audit and traceability
10. Standards-based interoperability
11. Extension points for modules, apps, and integrations
12. **Governed knowledge services**
13. **Conversational and guidance services**
14. A coherent unified experience shell

---

## 4. Role-Based Unified UI/UX Doctrine

> One coherent experience shell, one primary person identity, many possible roles, many
> lawful attached identifiers, many contexts, many applications, **many participation
> modes**, one governed environment.

"Role-based" includes regulated professional roles, caregiving roles, operational roles,
administrative roles, **wellness participation roles, community roles**, and non-clinical
consumer participation roles.

---

## 5. Broad Participation Doctrine

Impilo shall not be architected only around disease, diagnosis, facility care, or formal
professional workflows. The experience layer must support broad participation:

- Care and treatment
- Wellness and prevention
- **Fitness and physical activity**
- **Nutrition, diet, and lifestyle support**
- **Sleep, rest, and recovery support**
- **Clubs, communities, and peer groups**
- **Coaching and habit support**
- Consumer discovery and marketplace participation
- Purchase and fulfilment of lawful non-regulated goods and services
- Self-tracking, self-motivation, and health-adjacent life management
- Device-mediated and IoT-linked interactions

---

## 6. Wellness, Lifestyle, Fitness, Diet, and Sleep Doctrine

Wellness, lifestyle, fitness, diet, and sleep are **first-class domains** within the Impilo
experience layer and must not be treated as peripheral or cosmetic extensions.

The wellness domain should explicitly include support for how people **move, eat, sleep,
rest, recover, and live**. It must be broad enough to cover:

- Physical activity, exercise, and fitness
- Walking, running, and fitness clubs
- Finding safe places, routes, and communities for exercise
- Diet, nutrition, and healthy eating guidance
- Sleep patterns, sleep recovery, and sleep knowledge
- Habit and lifestyle support
- Wellness challenges, achievements, goals, and progress
- Coaching, motivation, and self-improvement
- Integration of wearable and device-linked activity streams
- Community and social participation

**Product doctrine**: The wellness experience should be highly usable, attractive,
motivating, and consumer-grade. It should embody:

- Low-friction onboarding
- Intuitive self-service
- Visually engaging progress and activity journeys
- Community and social participation
- Personalized and motivating experiences
- Strong mobile-first design
- High responsiveness and continuity across devices

---

## 7. Marketplace Doctrine

Marketplace participation shall be governed according to the risk, regulatory status, and
sensitivity of the product or service involved.

The Health Operating System must distinguish clearly between:

- Regulated clinical products and services
- Higher-risk health transactions
- Lower-risk and non-regulated products
- Wellness and lifestyle goods and services

**Doctrine: friction must be proportionate to risk.**

Low-risk items (bandages, wellness accessories, fitness goods, dietary support goods,
sleep-support goods) should be discoverable and purchasable through relatively low-friction
pathways. Sensitive transactions involving regulated products, prescriptions, claims, or
controlled goods must remain subject to stronger controls.

---

## 8. Graduated Trust and Friction Doctrine

Trust, verification, and friction must be **proportionate** to the sensitivity, risk, and
regulatory burden of the action:

| Action Type | Friction Level |
|---|---|
| Wellness exploration, club browsing, lifestyle content | Minimal |
| Browsing routes, dietary/sleep content, communities | Light-touch |
| Buying low-risk non-regulated goods | Straightforward |
| Connecting ordinary wellness devices | Low within safeguards |
| Accessing personal clinical records | Stronger assurance |
| Performing regulated professional work | Activated professional authority |
| Prescribing, claims, controlled fulfilment | Highest assurance |

---

## 9. Identity Doctrine

One person, one Health ID anchor, many attached identifiers of different classes, one
governed active context at a time.

The Health ID establishes personhood, continuity, and longitudinal participation across
time, roles, institutions, services, **wellness participation, diet and sleep experiences,
marketplace interactions, and device-mediated interactions**.

---

## 10. Progressive Identity Assurance Doctrine

Impilo shall operate a **progressive identity assurance model** in which persons and
professionals may enter through different enrollment pathways, each conferring a defined
trust level.

- A Health ID may be issued at first registration, including self-registration
- Its assurance status determines available access until further confirmation
- Higher-assurance states achieved through facility confirmation, registry matching,
  professional council validation, HR validation, or other approved means
- **Identity existence is separated from identity assurance**

---

## 11. Temporary Identity Conversion Doctrine

Every temporary ID shall display:

1. Its current assurance state
2. The reason for that state
3. The permissions currently available
4. The requirements for upgrade
5. The eligible upgrade pathways
6. The next best step

> Temporary identity should feel like a guided journey, not a dead end.

---

## 12. Emergency Help and Care-First Doctrine

> Care first, identity as soon as possible, reconciliation without loss.

No person may be denied urgent or emergency care because identity, registration, or
enrollment processes are incomplete. The system shall support governed emergency pathways
for temporary/unknown-person records with later reconciliation.

---

## 13. Temporary ID Abuse Prevention Doctrine

Temporary identifiers shall be: visibly marked, minimally privileged, reason-coded,
time-bound, attributable to a responsible creator, and subject to mandatory reconciliation,
supervisory review, and audit.

> Temporary IDs must help care, not help fraud.

---

## 14. Regulated Professional Participation Doctrine

> Sign in as a person; practice as a provider only under activated Provider ID.

---

## 15. Organizational and Operational Context Doctrine

Context includes: Facility ID, Department ID, Ward ID, Programme ID, Workspace ID, Shift
context, **Community catchment or outreach context, Marketplace context, Service-point
context, Club or community context, Device or IoT operating context**.

---

## 16. Multi-Class Identifier Doctrine

| Class | Purpose | Examples |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **Actor IDs** | Who is involved | Health ID, Provider ID, Staff ID, caregiver linkage |
| **Context IDs** | Where / under what setting | Facility ID, Department ID, Ward ID, Workspace ID, Programme ID, **Club/Community ID, Device operating context** |
| **Object IDs** | What thing / resource | Product ID, Medication ID, Asset ID, Equipment ID, Device ID, **IoT device ID, marketplace item ID, wellness-support object ID** |
| **Transaction IDs** | Specific action instances | Prescription ID, Order ID, Claim ID, **purchase transaction ID, booking ID, device-link transaction ID, wellness participation transaction ID** |
| **Record IDs** | Persistent information objects | Observation ID, Consent ID, Document ID, Care Plan ID, **wellness plan ID, sleep record ID, dietary record ID** |
| **Event IDs** | Discrete runtime events | Alert ID, Audit Event ID, **wellness activity event ID, sleep event ID, dietary intake event ID, IoT event ID** |

## 16a. Knowledge, Search, and Conversational Doctrine

The experience layer shall function as an active knowledge, guidance, search, and
conversational environment that can respond to the person, support decision-making,
surface relevant health information, and deliver timely prompts, reminders, warnings,
and recommendations appropriate to context, profile, preferences, and consent.

The experience layer must be:

- **Searchable** — users can look for health, wellness, lifestyle, diet, sleep, service,
  and marketplace information
- **Knowledge-enabled** — draws from governed repositories of health knowledge
- **Conversational** — users interact through natural dialogue, prompts, guided exchanges
- **Responsive** — talks back with relevant information, reminders, alerts, suggestions
- **Context-aware** — reflects who the person is, what is known, where they are, what
  services or risks apply, what they have opted into
- **Personalizable** — adapted to role, age, sex, life stage, conditions, medications,
  wellness goals, exposures, location, and other lawful context
- **Consent-aware** — prompts, nudges, and personalized guidance governed by the person's
  permissions, preferences, and applicable law or policy

The experience layer may also **proactively** surface useful, timely, and relevant
information — adherence support, screening reminders, follow-up prompts, campaign
outreach, environmental risk alerts, and healthy living encouragement.

---

## 17. Device and IoT Doctrine

Devices are **first-class entities** that may:

- Generate observations and stream activity/wellness data
- Support diet, sleep, activity, and recovery tracking
- Contribute environmental or exposure signals
- Trigger alerts, reminders, or workflow events
- Enrich personal, clinical, operational, or wellness experiences
- Support remote monitoring, telemetry, and continuity

---

## 18. Asset and Equipment Doctrine

- **Asset ID**: ownership, deployment, finance, governance, lifecycle perspective
- **Equipment ID**: specific operational device in use for care, diagnostics, monitoring

---

## 19. App and Extensibility Doctrine

Governed app ecosystem includes not only clinical and administrative modules, but also
**wellness, fitness, lifestyle, community, diet, sleep, device, IoT, and marketplace
modules**.

---

## 20. Access Control Doctrine

Every access decision must evaluate (where applicable):

1. Person identity (Health ID)
2. Active role
3. Attached role identifier (Provider ID, Staff ID)
4. Organizational affiliation
5. Facility or workspace context
6. Subject relationship
7. Purpose of use
8. Consent or legal basis
9. Assurance level
10. Transaction type
11. Workflow state
12. **Device or channel context**

---

## 21. Credentialing, Access, and Assurance Doctrine

> Trusted access must be strong enough for safety and light enough for real life.

---

## 22. Audit and Traceability Doctrine

Every significant action traceable to: person anchor, active role, role-linked identifier,
organizational context, facility/workspace, subject, transaction/record, app/module,
purpose of use, **device/channel context**, time/event context.

Includes clinical, operational, **wellness, diet, sleep, consumer, marketplace,
device-mediated, and IoT-mediated** actions in proportion to their sensitivity.

---

## 23. Design Consequences

1. One Health ID per person
2. Unified experience shell preserves identity continuity across roles
3. Role activation must be explicit and context-aware
4. Apps and modules feel native to one Health Operating System
5. Clear distinction between who, in what capacity, under what authority, on what subject,
   through which device, at what risk level
6. Broad life-linked participation with high-discipline clinical/regulatory control
7. **Wellness, fitness, lifestyle, diet, sleep, and community features are genuine product
   pillars, not decorative add-ons**
8. **Low-risk marketplace, wellness, and device functions remain easy and attractive**
9. **High-risk functions remain strongly governed**
